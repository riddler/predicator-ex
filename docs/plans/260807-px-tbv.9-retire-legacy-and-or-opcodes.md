# Retiring the Legacy `and`/`or` Opcodes Implementation Plan

## Overview

`["and"]` and `["or"]` are the two opcodes the compiler stopped emitting in
3.7.0 and the evaluator has kept running ever since. They execute
**non-short-circuiting** boolean logic - the opposite of what every other part
of the library now documents - and they exist only because ADR-0001 promised
that a stored instruction list is never invalidated. ADR-0003 kept that promise
but changed how it is discharged: by a version stamp plus an explicit upgrade
path, not by permanent acceptance.

This plan executes that retirement. It mints **ISA v3**, ships
`Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1` as the migration ADR-0003 requires, removes
the two evaluator clauses, and regenerates the conformance corpus onto the
frozen-expectation path px-t2v built for exactly this moment.

Beads issue: **px-tbv.9** (`area:evaluator`, `area:docs`, `release:4.0.0`).
Parent epic: **px-tbv** (Predicator 4.0.0). Blocks **px-tbv.5** (cuts the
release).

**The retirement mechanics are px-t2v's, not this plan's.** px-t2v landed the
`:removed_in` marker, `in_isa?/2`, `opcode_set/1`, `retired_in/1`, the §4
"Removed in" column, the generator's frozen-expectation path, and every test
binding that ties them together - deliberately with no opcode carrying a
marker, so that this bead is "data plus deletions" rather than a mechanism
design. Where the bead's own 2026-08-06 notes and the landed px-t2v spec
disagree, **the landed spec wins**; this plan is written against what is
actually in the tree.

## Desired End State

- `Predicator.Instructions.isa_version/0` returns `3`; `docs/isa.md` reads
  `Current version: **ISA v3**.`
- `"and"` and `"or"` carry `removed_in: 3` in `@opcodes`, keep their rows in
  §4's table and in the tier-1 names row, keep their `required_isa/1` answer of
  `{:ok, 1}` and their `tier/1` answer of `{:ok, 1}`, and have **no**
  `execute_instruction/2` clause.
- Evaluating a list containing either is refused with an `EvaluationError`
  whose reason is `"retired_opcode"` and whose message names the ISA version
  that removed the opcode and points at `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1` -
  discharging ADR-0003's "refused with a message naming the version it needs".
- `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1` rewrites a pre-3.7 flat `and`/`or` list
  into jump form, is the identity on a list containing neither, and returns a
  named error rather than a wrong answer on anything it cannot rewrite.
- The five `legacy.json` cases are still in `conformance/corpus/tier-1.json`
  with the same ids, the same tier, the same expectations, and a new `retired`
  feature tag; `manifest.json` and `registry.example.json` are regenerated
  onto the new `corpus_hash`.
- `CHANGELOG.md` carries one self-contained breaking-change bullet naming the
  pre-3.7 stored-artifact case.

Verification: full `mix quality` green, and `mix corpus.generate --check`
reporting no drift against the regenerated, checked-in corpus.

### Key Discoveries

- **A pre-3.7 artifact cannot contain a jump or a `make_list`** - those are ISA
  v2 opcodes shipped in 3.7.0, the same release that stopped emitting
  `and`/`or`. So the input domain `upgrade/1` must actually handle is
  **v1-only**, where every opcode's pop count is statically derivable from the
  instruction itself. This is what makes a correct rewrite tractable in this
  bead rather than a project of its own.
- **The refusal must live in the catch-all, not in a clause of its own.**
  `isa_sync_test.exs:189-191` asserts a retired opcode has no
  `execute_instruction/2` clause, and `parse_evaluator_opcodes/1` extracts
  literal backticked opcode heads - so the catch-all (whose head is the
  variable `unknown`) is invisible to it. Enriching the catch-all is both
  allowed and free on the happy path, because it is only reached once an
  instruction has already failed to match.
- **The upgrade is not answer-preserving, and that is ADR-0001's finding, not
  a new problem.** ADR-0001's Consequences already say "the short-circuit
  change is observable... a consumer relying on the error is relying on the
  bug". The three divergences are enumerated in Phase 1 and must be documented,
  not hidden.
- `registry.example.json` has **no generator task**; it is hand-maintained
  under a canonical-encoding test (`ratchet_registry_test.exs:94-105`) and two
  pin fields. Its update here is exactly two scalar edits.
- The `@opcodes` table has no pop-count column, and adding one would drag in a
  new `docs/isa.md` binding. `upgrade/1` therefore carries its own private pop
  table over `opcode_set(1)`, with a derived test asserting the key sets match.

## What We're NOT Doing

- **Not touching `lib/predicator/errors.ex`.** `operation_display_name/1`'s
  `:logical_and` / `:logical_or` clauses (`:126-127`) become unreachable from
  the evaluator but remain a correct, doctested display-name table keyed on an
  atom that the *parser's* AST still uses (`{:logical_and, ...}`). Removing
  them would add `area:api` and a conflict surface with px-tbv.1 for no
  behavioral gain.
- **Not touching the parser, the lexer, or the visitors.** The surface `AND` /
  `OR` operators and the `{:logical_and, _, _, _}` AST nodes are unaffected -
  they compile to jumps and have since 3.7.0. This bead removes an *opcode*,
  not an operator.
- **Not exposing `upgrade/1` on the `Predicator` façade.** The bead's
  acceptance criteria name `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1`. Widening
  `lib/predicator.ex` would add `area:api`, which this bead does not carry, and
  would collide with px-tbv.1's live worktree.
- **Not adding a whole-list pre-flight version check to `evaluate/3`.** That is
  an O(n) cost on every evaluation of a compile-once-run-many artifact, for a
  message the enriched catch-all delivers at zero happy-path cost. The
  consumer-facing pre-flight already exists and is documented:
  `opcode_set/1` (`docs/isa.md:46-51`).
- **Not changing `required_isa/1`'s return shape.** It keeps answering
  `{:ok, 1}` for a legacy list. §1 already specifies why that is correct and
  what supersedes it.
- **Not removing any conformance case, case id, or feature tag.** The five
  legacy cases stay; `legacy_logical` stays.
- **Not writing an ADR.** ADR-0001 and ADR-0003 both already govern this
  retirement by name.
- **Not touching `mix.exs`, `mix.lock`, or any `area:build` file.**
- **Not promoting `## [Unreleased]` to a version header.** That is px-tbv.5's
  work and requires an explicit release request.

## Implementation Approach

### The `upgrade/1` decision: ship it

**Settled: `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1` ships in this bead.** The bead's
acceptance criteria allow "shipped or its absence justified", but the landed
spec removes the second option:

- `docs/isa.md:35-37` - "Retirement mints the next ISA integer, in addition to
  taking a major library release **and an upgrade path**."
- `docs/isa.md:249-250` - "Retiring an opcode still requires an upgrade path
  (ADR-0003)."
- ADR-0003 `:113-121` - the stored-artifact guarantee "is delivered by the
  version stamp plus an **explicit upgrade path** - a function that rewrites an
  old list into current form... Retiring an opcode is permitted at a major
  version **when an upgrade path exists for it** (`px-tbv.9` is the first
  case)".

An absence justification would therefore not be a judgment call inside this
bead's discretion; it would be a retirement taken in violation of the rule the
immediately preceding bead wrote into the spec. Shipping is the only option
consistent with the tree.

The counter-argument worth answering, because the bead's notes raise it:
*ADR-0003 says refusal via `required_isa/1` is the safety story, so isn't
refusal enough?* No - ADR-0003 names refusal and the upgrade path as **two
halves of one guarantee**, not alternatives: "keeps producing the same answer,
**or** is refused with a message naming the version it needs". Refusal alone
leaves a consumer with stored artifacts holding data they can no longer run and
no supported way forward, which is the outcome ADR-0001's permanent-acceptance
rule existed to prevent and which ADR-0003 replaced rather than abandoned.

**Feasibility** rests on the v1-only observation above: a list old enough to
contain `["and"]` predates jumps and `make_list`, so a forward simulation over
statically known pop counts reconstructs the operand boundaries exactly. See
Phase 1 for the algorithm and for the four refusal cases that keep it honest
about its own limits.

### The refusal message

The bead's 2026-08-06 note asks that a stored pre-3.7 artifact be "refused with
a message naming the version it needs" rather than failing with
`unknown_opcode`. px-t2v discharged half of that by keeping the map row so
`required_isa/1` and `tier/1` keep answering. The other half is the evaluator's
message, and it goes in the catch-all clause: when an unmatched instruction's
head is a known-but-retired opcode, return reason `"retired_opcode"` with a
message naming the removing ISA version and `upgrade/1`. Every other unmatched
instruction keeps `"unknown_instruction"` unchanged.

### Phase ordering: `upgrade/1` before the retirement

`upgrade/1` lands first, while the legacy clauses still exist. That is not
arbitrary: it lets Phase 1's tests assert **equivalence** - that a legacy list
and its upgraded form produce the same answer across the boolean-only inputs
where the two semantics agree - which is only checkable while both paths run.
Phase 2 then adapts exactly those equivalence assertions, and the evidence
they produced is recorded rather than lost.

It also keeps each phase independently gate-green. Phase 1 is purely additive:
no opcode moves, `mix corpus.generate --check` reports no drift, and
`@isa_version` is still 2. Phase 2 is atomic by necessity - the marker, the
version bump, the clause deletion, the doc columns, and the corpus
regeneration all move `isa_sync_test` and `corpus_freshness_test` together, so
splitting them further would leave an intermediate gate red for reasons that
are not defects.

## Phase 1: `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1`

### Overview

Ship the migration ADR-0003 requires, additively, while both execution paths
still exist. Nothing about the ISA moves in this phase.

### Changes Required:

#### 1. The upgrade module

**File**: `lib/predicator/instructions/upgrade.ex` (new)
**Changes**: `@moduledoc false` implementation module. It is separate from
`instructions.ex` because the rewrite is ~120 lines against that module's
current ~316, and because a `@moduledoc false` module keeps the public API
surface exactly one function wider.

The algorithm is a **forward chunk simulation**, not a backward stack walk.
Maintain a stack whose elements are *sub-programs* (instruction lists), each
producing one value:

```elixir
# Each stack element is the sub-program that produces one value. For an
# ordinary opcode popping k values, the k chunks beneath it are concatenated
# in stack order and the instruction appended - which is exactly the
# instruction order that produced them. For ["and"]/["or"] the two chunks are
# spliced into jump form instead:
#
#   left ++ [["jump_if_falsy_or_pop", length(right) + 1]] ++ right
#
# The offset is relative and forward (docs/isa.md section 2): from the jump's
# own index it must land one past the right operand's last instruction.
# Nesting needs no special case - an inner and/or was already rewritten into
# the chunk this one is splicing, so its jump offsets are internal to that
# chunk and are unaffected by being moved.
```

The output is the concatenation of the final chunk stack in bottom-to-top
order, which preserves the "deeper stack at halt is not an error" rule
(`docs/isa.md:79-81`) exactly.

**The pop table**: a private `@pops` map over the ISA v1 opcode set, with
`call` and `make_list` reading their count from the operand. A comment binds it
to `docs/isa.md` §4's **Pops** column. A test asserts
`MapSet.new(Map.keys(@pops)) == Instructions.opcode_set(1)` via a small public
`@doc false` accessor, so a future v1-era opcode cannot be added to `@opcodes`
without this table noticing.

**The four refusals**, each an `EvaluationError` with reason
`"unsupported_upgrade"` and `operation: :upgrade`:

1. The list contains an ISA v2 opcode (`jump_if_falsy_or_pop`,
   `jump_if_true_or_pop`, `make_list`) **and** a retired opcode. A genuine
   pre-3.7 artifact cannot be in this state; re-targeting relative jumps across
   an insertion has no known consumer, and guessing is worse than refusing.
   Message says so.
2. Stack underflow - an opcode pops more chunks than the stack holds. The list
   was already unrunnable; `upgrade/1` says that rather than producing a
   silently different list.
3. An unknown opcode, or a `call` / `make_list` whose count operand is not a
   non-negative integer.
4. A malformed element (not a non-empty list headed by a binary), mirroring
   `required_isa/1`'s `"malformed_instruction"` distinction.

#### 2. The public function

**File**: `lib/predicator/instructions.ex`
**Changes**: `upgrade/1` delegating to the new module, plus a `## Migration`
paragraph in the moduledoc.

```elixir
@spec upgrade(Types.instruction_list()) ::
        {:ok, Types.instruction_list()} | {:error, EvaluationError.t()}
```

The `@doc` must carry, explicitly:

- **The identity guarantee.** A list containing no retired opcode is returned
  unchanged, so a consumer can run `upgrade/1` over every stored artifact
  unconditionally instead of pre-filtering.
- **The three semantic divergences**, because a migration that quietly changes
  answers is worse than no migration. Against the legacy opcodes, the upgraded
  list differs when and only when:
  1. **Short-circuiting.** The right operand is no longer evaluated once the
     left decides. Observable when the right operand errors or loads an unbound
     variable: legacy raised, upgraded returns the left value.
  2. **`:undefined` operands.** Legacy: `TypeMismatchError`. Upgraded:
     `undefined and x` is `:undefined`, `undefined or x` is `x`'s value
     (ECMAScript-aligned, ADR-0001).
  3. **A non-boolean *right* operand the left did not decide.** `true and 1`
     was a `TypeMismatchError` and is now `1`. A non-boolean **left** operand
     is still a `TypeMismatchError` at the jump, so the guard on the operand
     the expression actually depends on is unchanged.

  All three are the short-circuit change ADR-0001 shipped in 3.7.0 and called
  a bugfix; `upgrade/1` moves a stored artifact onto the semantics every
  source-compiled expression has had for two minor versions. Cite ADR-0001's
  Consequences rather than re-arguing it.
- **Doctests**: the identity case; `[["lit",true],["lit",false],["and"]]`
  upgrading to `[["lit",true],["jump_if_falsy_or_pop",2],["lit",false]]`; one
  nested case; one refusal.

#### 3. Tests

**File**: `test/predicator/instructions/upgrade_test.exs` (new)
**Changes**:

- **Equivalence** (the phase's centerpiece, and only writable now): for a table
  of boolean-only legacy lists - `and`/`or` over `true`/`false` in all four
  combinations, nested `(a and b) or c`, a comparison feeding an `and`
  (`evaluator_test.exs:383-396`'s composite is a good source) - assert
  `Evaluator.evaluate(legacy, ctx) == Evaluator.evaluate(upgraded, ctx)`.
- **Divergence**, asserted rather than avoided: one case per divergence above,
  pinning what each form returns, so Phase 2 keeps the upgraded half and the
  record of what changed survives.
- Structural: identity on a jump-form list; identity on a list with no logical
  opcode; correct offsets for a nested rewrite; multi-value final stack
  preserved in order.
- Each of the four refusals, asserting the reason and that the message names
  the cause.
- The pop-table-versus-`opcode_set(1)` set equality.

## Phase 2: Mint ISA v3 and retire the opcodes

### Overview

The atomic retirement: the marker, the version, the clause deletions, the
refusal message, the spec, and the corpus - all of which move
`isa_sync_test.exs` and `corpus_freshness_test.exs` together.

### Changes Required:

#### 1. The opcode table and the version

**File**: `lib/predicator/instructions.ex`
**Changes**: `@isa_version 2` -> `3` (`:37`), and:

```elixir
"and" => %{isa: 1, tier: 1, removed_in: 3},
"or" => %{isa: 1, tier: 1, removed_in: 3},
```

Nothing else in this file changes. `required_isa/1` keeps answering `{:ok, 1}`
for a legacy list by construction, which is what ADR-0003's refusal promise
rests on.

#### 2. The evaluator

**File**: `lib/predicator/evaluator.ex`
**Changes**:

- Delete the two `execute_instruction/2` clauses (`:402-410`) and both helper
  families, `execute_logical_and/1` (`:642-664`) and `execute_logical_or/1`
  (`:666-688`), specs and all.
- Moduledoc (`:8-13`): replace the sentence naming `and`/`or` as opcodes "this
  module still runs" with one stating that ISA v3 retired them, that a stored
  pre-3.7 artifact is refused with a message naming the version and pointing at
  `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1`, and that the ISA table
  (`docs/isa.md` §4) keeps their rows.
- Enrich the catch-all (`:508-516`) so a known-but-retired opcode gets its own
  message:

```elixir
# A retired opcode has no clause of its own (docs/isa.md section 4, "Retired
# opcodes") and lands here. It is not an *unknown* instruction - the ISA
# still has its row - so it gets the refusal ADR-0003 promises: a message
# naming the ISA version that removed it and the upgrade path. The lookup
# costs nothing on the happy path, because this clause is reached only after
# an instruction has already failed to match.
defp execute_instruction(%__MODULE__{}, [opcode | _operands] = unknown)
     when is_binary(opcode) do
  case Instructions.retired_in(opcode) do
    {:ok, version} when is_integer(version) -> retired_opcode_error(opcode, version)
    _live_or_unknown -> unknown_instruction_error(unknown)
  end
end
```

with the retired error carrying reason `"retired_opcode"` and
`operation: :evaluate`, and the message naming the opcode, the removing ISA
version, and `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1`. The original catch-all stays
as the final clause for malformed elements. Add the `Predicator.Instructions`
alias.

#### 3. `docs/isa.md`

**File**: `docs/isa.md`
**Changes**, none of which renumber or reorder a section:

- `:205-231` - `and`'s and `or`'s **Removed in** cells become `v3`. Every other
  cell in the table is untouched. **The tier-1 names row (`:190`) keeps both
  opcodes** - `isa_sync_test.exs:139-149` asserts set *equality* between the
  tier row and the map's tier assignment, and a retired opcode keeps its map
  entry, so removing it from the row is what would break.
- `:56` - `Current version: **ISA v2**.` -> `**ISA v3**.`
  (`isa_sync_test.exs:83-85` binds this line.)
- `:23-26` - the first §1 bullet's release history gains v3: minted by a
  retirement rather than an addition, which is the first time the integer moves
  without a new opcode. This also satisfies the max-version assertion at
  `:63-81`, which reads columns 5 and 8 - `v3` appears only in column 8, and
  that is the case that assertion was widened for.
- `:306-315` - §5's `and`/`or` bullet is rewritten. The semantics stay (they
  are what a v1 or v2 implementation must still provide), but the framing
  inverts: **retired at ISA v3**, no evaluator clause in this build, a v1/v2

  `"retired_opcode"`, and `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1` is the migration.
  Delete "A v2 implementation still has to run them - they are not deprecated

- `:463-466` - §7 gains a row:
  `| v3 | - | `and`, `or` | 4.0.0 |`, and the surrounding paragraph's "once any
  opcode has been retired" hedge becomes a statement of fact.

#### 4. The corpus

**File**: `conformance/cases/legacy.json`
**Changes**: rewrite the first case's `notes` - "accepted by the evaluator

## ISA Impact

1. **Version - bumps to ISA v3.** This is the first retirement and the first
   version minted by a removal rather than an addition
   (`docs/isa.md:35-39`). `@isa_version` moves to 3, `docs/isa.md`'s
   `Current version` line to `**ISA v3**`, and §7 gains a v3 row. It ships in
   the 4.0.0 major release, which is what a retirement requires (ADR-0003).
2. **Stamp** - no opcode subsection is added because no opcode is added. `and`
   and `or` keep their §4 rows, their tier-1 membership, and their §5
   semantics; their **Removed in** cells become `v3` and the §5 bullet is
   reframed as retired-but-still-required-of-v1/v2. The five conformance cases
   stay at tier 1 with frozen expectations and a `retired` tag.
3. **Migration** - **required and shipped.** An instruction list compiled
   before 3.7.0 containing `["and"]` or `["or"]` no longer runs on this build.
   It is refused with `"retired_opcode"` naming ISA v3, and
   `Predicator.Instructions.upgrade/1` rewrites it into jump form. The upgraded
   list is **not** answer-identical in three enumerated cases
   (short-circuiting, `:undefined` operands, a non-boolean right operand) -
   these are ADR-0001's 3.7.0 short-circuit change reaching stored artifacts,
   and they are documented on `upgrade/1` and in the changelog rather than
   glossed.

A sibling behind the current ISA version is an expected, documented state
(ADR-0003). Both siblings are ISA v1 today, and v1's opcode set contains
`and`/`or` permanently under the interval rule - so **this retirement obliges
the siblings to do nothing at all**, which is the interval rule delivering
exactly what it was chosen for.
